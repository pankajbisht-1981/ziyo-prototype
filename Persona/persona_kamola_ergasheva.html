A Day in Her Life
Kamola starts her morning by checking Telegram channels for job posts before her 9 am lecture. She saves three listings but two require 2+ years of experience she does not yet have. Between classes she opens LinkedIn — the interface is in English, the jobs are mostly abroad, and the local opportunities are buried in a feed she hasn't learned to filter. In the evening she works on a personal project for her GitHub portfolio, aware that employers will ask for one but unsure exactly what they want to see. She falls asleep with five browser tabs open: a Coursera course, a scholarship form in Russian, a company career page, and two unanswered questions — who is hiring in Tashkent right now, and how does someone like her get in front of them?
Behaviours & Habits
  • Monitors multiple Telegram channels and job boards daily — but finds them scattered and unreliable
  • Builds skills through Coursera, YouTube tutorials, and personal GitHub projects alongside her degree
  • Participates in hackathons and coding competitions to build her portfolio and network
  • Shares opportunities with university peers via group chats before acting on them herself
  • Prefers Uzbek or Russian content but defaults to English-language tools because local options are limited
  • Looks for credibility signals (company logos, official-looking listings) before trusting a source

Goals
  • Land a meaningful internship or entry-level tech role in Uzbekistan
  • Build a portfolio of real projects that proves her skills to employers
  • Develop practical, in-demand skills (web, mobile, data) alongside her degree
  • Grow a professional network and connect with mentors who can guide her career
What She Needs from Ziyo
  • One trusted platform that aggregates verified internship and job listings
  • Clear information on what skills and qualifications companies actually require
  • Guidance on CV writing and how to prepare for technical interviews
  • Access to scholarships, grants, and early-career development programmes
Challenges
  • Balancing university coursework with the time needed for job searching
  • Staying motivated when applications go unanswered for weeks
  • Knowing which skills to invest in when the market signal is unclear
  • Building credibility without industry experience to point to
Pain Points Today
  • Opportunities fragmented across dozens of channels — no single reliable source
  • Most listings require experience she does not yet have, no clear path for graduates
  • Limited transparency on salaries, culture, or what the role actually involves
  • English or Russian often required, creating a barrier for native Uzbek speakers
Key Design Insight: Kamola is not passive — she is actively searching every day. The problem is fragmentation, not motivation. Ziyo Forum's opportunity is to become the single trusted source she currently has to build herself from dozens of scattered channels.
