# Student Conversations — Ziyo Forum (Summary)

**Event:** Ziyo Forum (student event), Tashkent
**Date:** 9 October 2026 · two informal sessions (~41 min and ~18 min)
**Facilitators:** Anja (Germany) & Pankaj (India) · some students spoke via a translator
**Participants:** First- and third-year university students (engineering/design, Korean-language education, economics) and a group of 11th-grade school students; several drone/robotics enthusiasts.

> **Note on method:** auto-transcribed, informal, partly translated conversations. Findings are **directional/qualitative**, not statistically representative.

---

## Top takeaways

1. **Opportunities live on Telegram — and it's overwhelming.** Students follow *dozens* of channels (several said 10–30) for events, grants, and exchange programs. The recurring wish was a **single platform** that aggregates everything so they stop hunting "in every corner of the internet."
2. **Teachers are the real distribution channel.** Most school students came today because a teacher told them to; attendance is often *mandatory*, not chosen. Intrinsic interest is mixed — "interesting" for some, "a little boring" for others.
3. **No one can track what they've attended.** Telegram history disappears, and students keep no record of events or certificates — a clear gap between effort spent and provable credentials.
4. **Verified, recognized certificates would be highly valued.** Students add events to their CVs and get asked about them in interviews/admissions; a trusted, verifiable record "admission officers can easily check" resonated strongly.
5. **Friction kills follow-through.** Long multi-stage application forms (4–5 steps) cause drop-off — "that kind of makes me sick… I really do not want to apply if I see a lot of questions." Only the most motivated finish.
6. **Rewards/bonuses would drive repeat attendance.** Certificates, points toward university admission, or educational items (e.g., books) were named as motivators to come back.

---

## How students find opportunities

- **Telegram** = primary channel for opportunities, grants, and exchange programs (incl. "grant hunter"/exchange channels, and prep orgs like "top coach" and "Campus"-type projects for applying to top/Korean universities). Also used for chatting and scheduling.
- **Instagram** = mostly entertainment and inspiration; videos to "kill time." Less for formal opportunities.
- **Teachers & school/university channels** = a dominant source, especially for school students; often the *only* source and often compulsory.
- **Websites** = occasional, secondary.
- Many participants **had not heard of the Ziyo Forum** before being brought by a teacher.

## What they're looking for

- **Exchange programs, volunteering abroad, and summer programs** — especially short summer options, since students enrolled in Uzbek universities can't pause studies to go abroad.
- **Scholarships, grants, internships**, and guidance on *which universities/courses to apply to*.
- **Choice and focus:** school students feel overwhelmed studying ~20 subjects at once and want the ability to specialize / choose directions.
- **Entrance-exam prep** (math, English) via commercial learning centers — competition is intense; prep starts 2–5 years before university.

## Pain points

- **Fragmentation:** too many channels; information scattered; "lazy to find" it all → desire to consolidate into one app ("would save a lot of space on my Telegram as well").
- **Application friction:** long, multi-stage forms deter applicants.
- **No attendance/credential tracking:** Telegram loses history; no personal log of events attended or certificates earned.
- **Motivation gap:** participation driven by teacher mandate and scholarship rules more than interest; content feels boring when not relevant to the student's field.

## Scholarship & credential context (local universities)

- Scholarships must be **re-earned yearly**: maintain GPA, stay *socially active*, read books, do research, write and **defend** articles/papers in seminars.
- **AI/ChatGPT use is penalized** — flagged work must be redone.
- Social activity and event attendance count toward keeping the scholarship — a structural reason events matter to students.
- Attended events go on CVs/resumes; interviewers and admissions may ask about them.

---

## Implications for Ziyo

- **Be the aggregator.** The strongest, most repeated signal: one place for events + scholarships + grants + courses + universities, reducing dependence on scattered Telegram channels.
- **Issue verifiable certificates / an attendance record.** A trusted, institution-recognized log of what a student attended and achieved directly answers the tracking gap and the CV/admissions use case. (Ties naturally to the ZiyoCoin/rewards and profile work in the app prototype.)
- **Reward participation.** Points toward admission, certificates, or educational items (books) were named as motivators — pair extrinsic rewards with genuinely relevant content.
- **Minimize application friction.** Short, few-step flows; pre-filled profiles; one-tap apply where possible.
- **Win teachers as a channel.** Teachers already drive turnout — equip them to surface events and let attendance feed a student's verified record.
- **Personalize by interest.** Boredom came from irrelevance; recommend by field (robotics, economics, design, languages) and surface summer/exchange options prominently.

## Representative voices (paraphrased from transcript)

- *"They want to collect them in one place because they're lazy to find them in every corner of the internet."*
- *"I have tons of channels on Telegram… if I just had access to one platform where all the activities and programs are listed, I'd click and apply. That would save a lot of time — and space on my Telegram."*
- *"It requires a lot of stages — 4 or 5 — and that kind of makes me sick. I don't want to apply if I see a lot of questions."*
- *"A verified certificate would be beneficial for applying to universities, because admission officers can easily check it."*
- *"We're required to improve our social activity at university in order to keep our scholarship."*
- On attending today: one student found it *"interesting,"* another thought it *"a little boring."*
